summary: An animated map of every Canadian federal election since 1867, showing how each province and territory split its House of Commons seats between parties.
problem: >-
  Election maps make results easy to read, but they usually show one election
  at a time, so the bigger story is lost: how support for each party rises and
  falls in each province over decades. The assignment asked for an interactive
  piece about time and space, and Canada's full election history fits both.
solution: >-
  A single-page map of Canada with a column over each province and territory,
  split into party segments sized by the seats each party won there. Pick any of
  the 44 elections from 1867 to 2021 and the columns animate to the new result,
  so you can watch a region's support shift from one election to the next.

highlights:
  - Researched the seats won by every party in every province and territory across all 44 federal elections, entered them in a spreadsheet, and converted it to JSON.
  - Built the page in SvelteKit and Tailwind CSS, with a column over each province and territory on the map and a stacked segment for each party, sized by its share of that region's seats.
  - Animated every change of election year with GSAP, so segments grow, shrink and change colour to show support rising and falling.
  - Kept each year accurate to its time, with a legend listing only the parties that ran and empty columns for provinces that hadn't yet joined Confederation.
