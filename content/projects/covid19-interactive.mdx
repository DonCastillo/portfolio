summary: A seven-scene interactive animation in Processing that explains COVID-19, from where it started to how it spreads, with each scene revealed through the mouse or keyboard.
problem: >-
  The final project's theme was COVID-19, and the brief asked for more than a
  technical exercise: a program that reveals something about the pandemic, or
  about how we were living through it, in late 2020.
solution: >-
  An interactive piece in seven scenes, each teaching one fact. You click the
  red dot where the outbreak began, move the mouse to bring hidden particles
  into view, put a mask on a crowd, and watch a simulated outbreak spread from
  one infected person.

highlights:
  - Designed seven scenes that each pair one COVID-19 fact with an interaction, from clicking the outbreak's origin on a map to moving the mouse to watch the virus's crown-like spikes appear.
  - Simulated transmission with particles, where each infected particle can infect up to two others on contact, to show what a reproduction number of about 2 looks like.
  - Built the scenes from reusable classes for the virus, people, particles and text boxes, in about 1,400 lines of Processing.
  - Used a flashlight effect, adapted from Processing's brightness example and credited, to reveal particles lurking on everyday surfaces.
